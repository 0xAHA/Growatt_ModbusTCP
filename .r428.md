Confirmed, fixed on `main`, and your diagnosis was right. The cause turned out to be slightly
different from what you proposed, in a way that matters for one more case.

## Your reading of the registers is correct

From your diagnostics:

```
battery_type    = 3      (Lithium)
bat_low_to_uti  = 500    -> 50.0
ac_to_bat_volt  = 750    -> 75.0
```

So the **values were never wrong** — 50 % and 75 % are exactly the thresholds you observe the
inverter acting on. Only the unit and the range were.

Your reasoning from the bank voltage is what makes this conclusive rather than probable: a 48 V
nominal bank reading 53 V cannot have a 75 V switchover threshold, so 75 could only be a
percentage.

## The cause was not a missing battery-type check

`battery_dependent: True` is already set on both registers, and Lithium is already value 3.
The logic existed and should have applied.

What was wrong is that it ran **once, in the entity's constructor**, and froze the result as
fixed attributes. It never recomputed.

That matters beyond your case, because **battery type is itself a writable control in this
integration**. Anyone switching their battery type to Lithium would have kept volts and 20-64
until they restarted Home Assistant — and would have had no reason to connect the two.

So the fix is your suggestion, made live: unit, minimum, maximum and step are now resolved from
the current battery type each time they are read, rather than at creation. Lithium gives
**% and 0-100**; lead-acid, flooded and the self-defined types keep **V and 20.0-64.0**.

## What you should see

After upgrading, both entities should show **%**, accept anything from 0 to 100, and stop
refusing 75. Your existing values need no correction — they are already the numbers you want.

If either still shows V, please say so and include the battery type the integration reports,
because that would mean the type is not reaching the entity at all, which is a different fault.
